A personal memory

THE FIRST TIME
I SAW YOU PLAY.

Words by Annapurani Vaidyanathan

2006. My first-ever memory of watching you play. It was a Wimbledon final against Nadal. I was staring at a 24-year-old star who already had 7 grand slams in his kitty. A ball boy who had worked hard to earn et al.

The numbers kept growing. 7 became 17. But you didn’t lose sheen.

You became the idol, you became the GOAT. You became a legend that didn’t shy away when he wanted to emote.

You are mocked for your tears, but they push us to relate to you more. They show how much each match mattered to you and why you’ll probably be the only legend I’ll gush about and adore. They make you vulnerable and human, and one of us – not just a celeb who stands on a pedestal.

Seven summers later, in 2013, you were down with injury. Critics began to write you off; they said 32 was a good age to hang up your boots. They said it was time for you to call it a day. Slams, they said, wouldn’t anymore be your forte.

They began to bid you goodbye, but you didn’t care. You simply chose to hang in there. You stumbled, you fell. Yet you rose with grace every single time, and had the world again under your spell.

You bounced back in 2017, and how! At 35, you scourged every raised eyebrow. You returned from six months away from the tour and won the Australian Open. You showed the world why tennis will always be your jam, why you won’t let yourself be easily outrun.

You made the world run short of superlatives, you made words fail critics. When people put you on the back burner, you came back stronger. You proved to them that age is just another number.

You defied full stops. You taught us to look beyond pitfalls and barriers. You gave us reason to believe, to stay undaunted, to keep going, to forge new forevers.

When the numbers become a story

OH, BUT THE
NUMBERS DO MATTER.

Words by Annapurani Vaidyanathan

From your first ATP Title in 2001, at Milan, to reaching the semifinals at the 2020 Australian Open, from capturing your Career Grand Slam in 2009, to fending off bulletins that scream ‘retire now thou can’, from stepping into the field as a ball boy to figuring out your own paradigms, from outshining peers in your era to growing to become the greatest of all time, you made tennis a celebration, a ride that comes doused with mellifluous chimes.

You always worked your fingers to the bone for every micron of your sheen, be it clinching your first slam in 2003 or winning 20 in 2018, be it breaking into the top 100 when you were just 18, or finding your breakthrough – beating Sampras on Centre Court at 19, be it the aces, the drop shots, the flawless slice you bring into play, or the backhand that looks like a perfectly choreographed ballet, the world knows now that your perfection couldn't have been forged in a day.

You made the game human; you suffused your every minute on the court with passion, heck, with you in them, even press conferences seemed fun.

Tennis – to me – will always be synonymous with your name, for the sport always shimmers better through the prism of your game.

Beyond the numbers

WHY WE
KEPT WATCHING.

Words by Annapurani Vaidyanathan

50 years from today, the world will be a different place. But there still will be players in scores and fans in millions who will look up to your calibre, your demeanour, your inexplicable grace. Your name will continue to reverberate the loudest in stadiums, your matches will continue to leave the audience in a daze, Because 50 years from today…. you will still be ahead of the race.

For it wasn’t just the slams that made you the GOAT. It wasn’t just the ranking that kept you afloat.

You stood out because, in an era where we are always engulfed with reasons to stop, you showed us how to keep going, come storm, sunshine or rainfall. You showed us how human you are and how flawed, you showed us that you might break down and fall, but you will get back up again as many times as you need to, to stand tall.

You showed us what it takes to follow your heart, to do what you love and to do it for as long as you last. You showed us how to prove the world wrong when it writes you off, when it thinks you are a thing of the past.

Maybe that is why every time you won, it became a victory so profoundly monumental. Maybe that is why watching you lose felt like a punch in the gut, a loss so unapologetically personal. Maybe that is why watching you play will always be a vision to behold, a memory to cherish, a treasure to relish when clouds turn grey. Maybe that is why I wake up each day, dreaming of seeing your magic again on the hard court, on grass and on clay!

A final word

YOU AREN'T
HISTORY.

Words by Annapurani Vaidyanathan

You aren’t history – you’re the present, you’re the legacy that keeps the future growing. You define what good looks like, you show people how to keep the ball rolling. You will never be referred to in tenses past – in did, had, or was. Because, be it on court or outside, you always keep reaching for the stars.

It’s been 3 years, 11 months, 5 days since you bid goodbye, yet - to date - every time you set foot in them, stadiums continue to erupt. Because in you, we all trust, and from you, we learn what it takes to trust our gut.

You’re the benchmark that players measure themselves against, it is your validation they strive for. Every time we see you, you leave us in a daze, for in our eyes, you will always be the GOAT, the star!
